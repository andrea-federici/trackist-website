How it is calculated

Your PB gives your race pace: the PB time divided by the PB distance. 100% is that pace. A higher percentage is faster: 110% means 10% more speed than your PB pace.

rep time = rep distance × (PB time ÷ PB distance) ÷ (% ÷ 100)

This is the same % of PB as in the rep pace calculator, so the two tools give the same numbers.

A percentage of speed, not of time

90% of the speed is not 10% more time: it is 11.1% more. A 60.0 s 400 m at 90% is 66.7 s, not 66.0 s. Some coaches use a percentage of time instead. If yours does, their numbers will be slightly different.

Reading the table

The 100% row is your race pace, and it is highlighted. How hard a percentage is depends on the rep distance. 110% of 5000 m pace over 400 m is a solid session for a distance runner. 110% of 400 m pace over 400 m is faster than the PB itself, so it is not possible.

Worked example

An 800 m runner with a PB of 2:00.00 has a race pace of 15.0 s per 100 m, which is 60.0 per 400 m. A 400 m rep at 100% is 60.0, at 110% it is 54.5 and at 90% it is 66.7. A 200 m rep at 120% is 25.0.

Limits

Percentages of one PB do not tell you how fast you can race other distances. For that, use the race time predictor. The table does not choose the session for you: how many reps, how much recovery and which percentage is up to you and your coach, or your plan.

Questions

How do I calculate a percentage of my PB pace?

Divide your PB time by its distance to get seconds per metre. Multiply by the rep distance, then divide by the percentage. With a 2:00 800 m PB, a 400 m at 90% is 400 × 0.15 ÷ 0.9 = 66.7 seconds.

Is 90% of PB pace the same as 10% slower?

No. The percentage is of speed. 90% of the speed takes 11.1% more time, not 10%. A 60.0 second lap at 90% is 66.7 seconds, not 66.0.

Can I print the table?

Yes. Use the Print the table button. The printed page has only the table and your PB.

Why do the rep distances change with the event?

Each event shows a range of rep distances around it: shorter for sprinters, longer for distance runners. A 100 m PB shows 60 m to 300 m. A 5000 m PB shows 400 m to 2000 m.
