Where your training diary is stored

StrideBuddy requires an account. Its server database is the main copy of your training diary and latest Daily Review. The app also keeps a copy on your device so you can log and read your diary offline. The account diary includes your athlete profile, personal bests, workouts, planned sessions, planned races, notes, reps, splits, recoveries, strength entries, and latest Daily Review. It lets your devices restore and sync the same diary and review.

Existing users may also have an older copy in their private iCloud account. To use the server-storage version, an existing user must accept the current Terms and Privacy Policy, sign in, and complete a verified migration. StrideBuddy compares stable entry IDs, counts, and content hashes before the server database becomes the main copy.

If verification fails, the diary remains saved on the device and in private iCloud. The server-storage version stays at the retry and support screen until migration succeeds. It does not continue in the older local and iCloud mode. After a successful migration, the old iCloud records are kept unchanged as a recovery archive. They are not used as the active diary and are not automatically deleted.

StrideBuddy uses the data you enter for logging, diary views, workout categorization, personal-best tracking, Daily Review, and future AI features.

Optional product analytics

StrideBuddy asks whether you want to share a small amount of product-usage data. Product analytics is off until you choose Share usage data. Choosing Not now does not limit the app, and you can change the separate Product Analytics setting at any time in the You tab. This choice is independent from the AI setting.

If you enable product analytics, the app sends eight kinds of interaction events to PostHog’s EU Cloud service. These events may show that an app session started, a logging flow started, a logging mode was selected, a logging flow was left, a workout save succeeded or failed, the post-save Logbook Moment appeared, or a history surface such as Diary, workout detail, Daily Review, or records was opened.

Analytics events may include a random analytics-only identifier, app version and build, development, TestFlight, or production environment, logging mode, entry source, today-versus-past date category, safe template category, logging-flow stage, a normalized save-failure code, broad count buckets, and a safe Daily Review availability state. The random identifier is separate from the identifier used by AI infrastructure. StrideBuddy does not use PostHog person profiles and does not identify you by name or contact details.

Product analytics never includes workout notes, Quick Log text, generated review text, athlete names, contact information, exact personal bests, workout times, splits, recoveries, distances, workout structures, RPE, feelings, injury information, body-signal tags, race results, venue or precise location, free-form error messages, AI prompts or outputs, security material, advertising identifiers, or IDFA. Session replay, tap autocapture, screen recording, crash capture, application-log capture, surveys, feature flags, and advertising tracking are disabled.

PostHog receives normal network connection data, such as an IP address, while accepting a request. Before product analytics is enabled in a release, StrideBuddy configures the EU project to discard stored client IP addresses and disables GeoIP enrichment, so location properties are not added to the event record. PostHog processes analytics for StrideBuddy under its applicable terms, privacy commitments, and data-processing terms. You can read the PostHog Privacy Policy.

Information StrideBuddy processes

The information StrideBuddy processes depends on how you use the app and may include:

  • training diary content stored on your device and in StrideBuddy’s account database, including workouts, notes, RPE, feeling, tags, races, personal bests, reps, splits, recoveries, and strength entries;
  • profile and event details, including your competition category, event, athlete type, timezone, and event-specific personal-best context;
  • the latest Daily Review generated for each local day and language, stored in your account so your devices can read the same review;
  • account and sign-in details, including an internal StrideBuddy user ID, Firebase user ID, Sign in with Apple provider subject, device-install ID, accepted policy versions, and account state;
  • data required for an AI request, but only when AI features are enabled and need to make a request;
  • the optional, limited product-interaction, fitness-category, identifier, and diagnostic data described in the Product Analytics section, but only after you enable it;
  • technical and security information, including pseudonymous install identifiers, request identifiers, source hashes, rate-limit counters, salted IP hashes, app version, locale, and policy-acceptance metadata; and
  • messages you send to StrideBuddy for support, feedback, privacy requests, or deletion requests, along with details such as your email address.

AI features

AI features may run automatically after you confirm that you are at least 18 and accept StrideBuddy’s terms, privacy policy, and AI disclosure. When a feature runs, the app sends only the information that feature needs to StrideBuddy’s backend. The backend then sends the relevant prompt data to Google’s paid Gemini API to generate a response.

Depending on which feature runs, the request may contain:

  • a pseudonymous app-install identifier and a request identifier used only once;
  • your local date and timezone, plus a source hash that checks whether the underlying diary input has changed;
  • your event profile, which may include event distance, sex, and personal-best times;
  • the workout under review and brief summaries of recent workouts, including dates, workout type, title, summary, category, RPE, feeling, notes, and facts such as distance, duration, race time, exercise names, or obstacle-work details;
  • a brief summary created on your device from recent weeks, which may include session and category counts, recent race logs, repeated body-signal terms from notes, average RPE, and low-feeling counts; and
  • for Daily Review, brief context from earlier Daily Reviews when it helps relate a new workout to your recent training.

You write your own notes, so they may contain health information or other sensitive details if you choose to add them. Do not put anything in your notes that you do not want AI features to process.

StrideBuddy does not send your complete diary history to Gemini. Before anything is sent, the app reduces recent history to a short summary on your device. StrideBuddy uses the paid Google Gemini API. Google states that it does not use data submitted to its paid Gemini API services to train Google AI models. Google may process Gemini API input and output under its applicable terms and privacy commitments, including the Gemini API Additional Terms and the Google Privacy Policy.

AI disclosure

Daily Review and future AI features use selected diary data to generate text. That text may be wrong or incomplete. StrideBuddy does not provide medical advice, diagnoses, injury-risk scores, emergency support, or a guaranteed training plan. It should not be your only source for decisions about training, health, injuries, or safety.

After onboarding, you can control every AI feature with one master toggle in the You tab. Turning it off prevents future AI requests. You can still log workouts and view your diary on your device.

Account, backend, and security data

StrideBuddy’s backend stores your account-owned diary data. It also uses technical data to sync changes, run AI features, apply generation limits, prevent abuse, and block malicious traffic. That data may include:

  • your internal account ID, sign-in provider mapping, and pseudonymous app-install identifiers;
  • stable diary-entry IDs, server revisions, sync cursors, deletion markers, and mutation receipts used to prevent duplicate or lost changes;
  • request identifiers and source hashes;
  • timezone information for checking review windows;
  • counters that enforce limits on AI requests;
  • limited metadata about your acceptance of AI terms, including accepted policy versions, app version, locale or region when available, adult confirmation, and whether AI is enabled; and
  • a salted hash created from the request’s IP address for abuse prevention. Backend rate-limit records keep the hash rather than the raw IP address.

The account database stores the latest Daily Review for each local day and language. Your devices receive that stored review through diary sync. When the diary input has not changed, the backend returns the existing review instead of generating another one. A newer Daily Review for the same day and language replaces the earlier one. Other AI output remains stored on your device unless this policy says otherwise.

StrideBuddy processes information for the following purposes:

  • to create and secure your account, sync your diary, restore it on another device, and resolve competing offline changes without relying on device clocks;
  • to generate Daily Review and future AI features when AI is enabled;
  • to remember whether you accepted the Terms, Privacy Policy, and AI disclosure;
  • with your separate choice, to understand activation, repeated logging, reliability, historical-value discovery, and retention;

  • to answer support, privacy, deletion, or legal requests; and
  • to comply with the law, enforce the Terms, and protect rights, safety, and security.

Retention

StrideBuddy keeps the server diary and latest Daily Reviews while the account is active. Deleted entries remain as limited deletion markers so an offline device cannot restore them by mistake. When you delete the account, the active server diary, Daily Reviews, and deletion markers are removed.

Render continuously backs up the paid account database for point-in-time recovery. The current recovery window is three days. Data deleted from the active database may remain in those recovery copies until that window expires. Recovery copies are used only to restore the service after data loss. A restored database must reapply any later account deletions before account access resumes.

An older iCloud archive remains in your Apple Account until you remove StrideBuddy’s iCloud data through Apple’s settings. StrideBuddy does not automatically delete that private archive. Backend rate-limit records are usually kept for about 24 hours. StrideBuddy may keep limited security records longer when necessary to protect the service, investigate abuse, comply with the law, or resolve security problems.

Optional PostHog analytics events are intended to be kept for no more than 12 months. StrideBuddy will configure that period where the service provides a retention control or use PostHog’s deletion process to enforce it. A consent record and the random analytics identifier remain on your device until the app or its data is deleted or the analytics identity is reset.

If you enable product analytics, the limited events described above go to PostHog EU Cloud. An older iCloud archive is handled by Apple under the Apple Privacy Policy. These providers process data for StrideBuddy under their applicable terms and privacy commitments.

In a business transfer, StrideBuddy will require the recipient to handle personal information in line with this policy unless you receive notice and any choices required by law.

Your control

You control your diary through the app. You can use the on-device export, sign out, remove downloaded account data when no change is waiting to sync, or delete your account in Settings. You control any older iCloud archive through your device’s iCloud settings. AI processing is part of StrideBuddy’s core experience. A master toggle in the You tab controls all AI features. Turning it off stops future AI requests but leaves logging and diary viewing available.

Product Analytics has its own control in the You tab. Turning it off stops future analytics transmission. It does not turn off AI, change local features, or delete analytics events that PostHog already received. You may also request deletion of those existing events by contacting StrideBuddy.

Your location may give you privacy rights, including rights to access, correct, delete, or object to the processing of your data. A signed-in account lets StrideBuddy delete the correct server diary and the operational backend records linked to its registered installations. We may still need your analytics identifier to delete optional PostHog events.

StrideBuddy does not use product analytics for decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects about you. The analytics only informs aggregate product and reliability decisions.

Deletion and requests

You can start account deletion at the bottom of Settings without emailing support. StrideBuddy asks you to sign in with Apple again and confirm the permanent action. The server diary, Daily Reviews, account mapping, registered installations, App Attest keys, sync records, and operational AI or security records linked to those installations are then removed. StrideBuddy also asks Firebase and Apple to revoke or delete the related sign-in identity where their systems allow it. A provider cleanup failure does not stop deletion of StrideBuddy data.

Deleting the account does not automatically delete an older private iCloud archive, iOS backup, or export that you control. You can remove iCloud data through Apple’s settings and remove downloaded account data from the device in the app. Database backups age out under the backup schedule. To request deletion of PostHog analytics events or support messages, contact StrideBuddy.

StrideBuddy will answer privacy requests within the period required by applicable law. Some limited information may be kept when necessary for security, legal compliance, or dispute resolution.
