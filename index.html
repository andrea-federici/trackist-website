Why StrideBuddy

Track sessions don’t fit neatly into generic running apps.

Your diary should understand reps, recoveries, splits, and the reason behind the session.

Track training

The whole track session

A track session can include reps, sets, recoveries, split times, race pace, and how the work felt. Those details belong in the log.

Notes apps

Easy to write, hard to revisit

Notes can hold the session, but they don’t give it enough structure to compare workouts or review a season.

GPS running apps

Good at routes, less useful on the track

Most running apps are designed around GPS runs, so a structured session built for your event gets flattened into distance and pace.

StrideBuddy records a session in the same terms you and your coach use at the track.

Features

Keep the session details without slowing down.

StrideBuddy knows that a tempo run and a set of 300s are different kinds of work.

Log the session as it happened

Build sessions from blocks, reps, splits, and recoveries, including active jogs and floats. You can also mark any rep as hurdles or steeple.

Five ways to start a log

Choose continuous, structured, strength, or race logging. For anything that doesn’t fit those four, use the free-text Quick Log. They all open from the same Log button.

Strength logging that stays out of the way

Tap exercises to add them, or begin with a template. Sets, reps, and load are there when you need them, but you don’t have to fill in a spreadsheet first.

Add as much detail as you need

The basic log is quick. Open Optional Signals when you want to add effort, how the session felt, weather, surface, or other feel tags.

A curve for speed, a ledger for times

Your signature speed curve covers the distances you run, while the ledger breaks them down by distance. Switch between one season and all time, with reps and races shown side by side and never scored.

A review of every workout

Daily Review looks at each workout you log and gives you a practical read on the session in the context of your recent training.

Logging

Log the work while it’s still fresh.

One place to start

Choose continuous, structured, strength, race, or a quick note from one menu.

Strength

Build the session by tapping exercises instead of filling out a long form.

Structured

Keep the blocks, reps, splits, and recoveries that define the workout.

Review

Finish the log, then read the session back.

Daily Review runs after you save a workout. It uses the session, your notes, your event profile, and a compact view of recent training to give you specific feedback without reducing the workout to a score.

Records brings your fastest reps and races onto one page. A hand-drawn signature curve follows the distances you run, and the ledger lists your times by distance. You can look at a single season or your full history.
