Why Trackist

Track workouts are structured. Generic apps aren’t.

Your training has reps, recoveries, splits, and intent. The tools you use should too.

The way you train

Reps, sets, and recoveries

Track athletes train in reps, sets, recoveries, split times, race pace, and session feel — not just distance and pace.

Notes apps

Flexible, but a dead end

A notes app will hold anything you type, but it’s impossible to review, compare, or make sense of over a season.

GPS running apps

Built for roads, not lanes

Generic running apps understand GPS runs. They don’t understand a structured track session built around an event.

Trackist gives athletes a diary built around their actual training language.

Features

Everything a track diary should be.

A clean, fast log that knows the difference between a tempo run and a set of 300s.

Structured workout logging

Build sessions with blocks, reps, splits, recoveries, RPE, feeling, and notes — in distance or time modes.

Continuous, strength & race logs

One diary for easy runs, structured track sessions, gym work, and race results.

Event-specific categorization

Trackist names and categorizes sessions around your primary event — from sprint work to aerobic quality.

Personal bests & workout PRs

Keep race PBs and training bests connected to the diary, so progress stays in context.

Today & Diary workflow

Quick-log from Today, then review everything in a clean weekly training ledger.

Shareable workout stickers

Turn completed sessions into clean share images made for athletes.

How it works

Three steps, then on with your day.

1

Log the session

Choose the workout type and enter the details that matter — reps, splits, recovery, feel.

2

Let Trackist structure it

Trackist stores the session, names it, and categorizes it around your event.

3

Review your training

Use Today, Diary, PBs, and workout details to understand your work over time.
